Central Yiddish

Ashkenazi Yiddish

The most widely spoken Yiddish dialect today. Used by Hasidic communities in the United States, Israel, and worldwide. Characterized by distinct vowel patterns that differ from Litvish (e.g., toyre vs. tayre).

Northeastern Yiddish

Litvish Yiddish

The YIVO standard dialect, originating in Lithuania, Belarus, and Latvia. Used as the academic standard for Yiddish writing. Distinct vowel system — considered the prestige dialect in secular Yiddish culture.

Southeastern Yiddish

Galician Yiddish

Spoken historically in Galicia (modern-day Poland and western Ukraine). Shares features with Ukrainian Yiddish. Vocabulary and pronunciation differ from both Ashkenazi and Litvish, with unique Slavic borrowings.

Why Dialect-Aware Transcription Matters

The same Yiddish word can be pronounced differently across dialects. For example, the word "Torah" is pronounced toyre in Ashkenazi/Hasidic Yiddish and tayre in Litvish. A transcription tool unaware of these differences produces errors that are difficult to correct.
